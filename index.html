Abstract

Tool-using language model agents can behave unsafely at intermediate steps even when their final answer appears acceptable. A trajectory may contain an unsafe tool call, a leaked value, obedience to an injected instruction, harmful compliance, or an unnecessary refusal of a benign task. FATE addresses this trajectory-level safety problem by turning the agent's own verifier-scored failures into repair supervision. For each failed rollout, the current policy proposes multiple corrected trajectories. These candidates are then re-evaluated for security, task utility, refusal calibration, and trajectory validity. Feasible candidates are filtered through a Pareto-front selection rule, producing balanced repair targets without relying on expert-written demonstrations. The selected repairs are used for supervised warmup and Pareto-aware policy optimization, allowing the policy to improve from its current failure distribution while preserving useful behavior.

Framework

FATE turns failed agent trajectories into verifier-filtered repair supervision.
